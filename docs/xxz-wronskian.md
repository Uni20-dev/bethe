# Testing odd-ring XXZ polynomials independently

[Odd-ring continuation](xxz-odd-continuation.md) | [Polynomial equations](xxz-polynomial.md)

The internal `check_odd_wronskian<Real>` in
[xxz_wronskian.hpp](../include/bethe/xxz_wronskian.hpp) supplies an independent
coefficient test for odd periodic rings at `-1<Delta<=0`. It does not change
the public solver domain. Its `consistent` status is a numerical statement,
**not** an unconditional physical-state or ground-state certificate.

## Why another equation?

[Bajnok et al., section 3](../CITATIONS.md#bajnok-2020) relate XXZ admissibility
to a second solution of the Baxter equation, using a quantum Wronskian.
Their construction assumes generic `q=exp(i*gamma)`, not a root of unity.
For odd N the second solution is a Laurent polynomial; even N can require
an additional logarithmic term. Their finite, distinct-root hypotheses and
singular-pair condition matter: solving a cleared Bethe equation alone is
not enough.

The identity to check is

```text
(t-t^-1)^N = P(t*q^(1/2))*Q(t*q^(-1/2))
           -P(t*q^(-1/2))*Q(t*q^(1/2)),
q=exp(i*gamma), Delta=cos(gamma), t=exp(lambda).
```

The code restricts N to odd values. It does not try to detect whether a
floating-point coupling is mathematically a root of unity, and never treats
numerical consistency as proof that the theorem's hypotheses hold.

## From the solver's coefficients to the Wronskian

Our scaled rapidity is `z=a*tanh(lambda)`, where
`a=sqrt((1+Delta)/(1-Delta))`. Thus

```text
X=t^2=(a+z)/(a-z),
z=a*(X-1)/(X+1).
```

For a monic input polynomial `F(x)` with `z=o+s*x`, homogenized Horner
evaluation constructs

```text
G(X) = [s*(X+1)]^M * F(((a-o)*X-(a+o))/(s*(X+1))).
Q(t) = t^-M * G(t^2).
```

This transformation avoids extracting colliding roots and does not divide
by the endpoint values `F((+-a-o)/s)`. A common positive scale in the Horner
factors limits intermediate growth. The largest absolute coefficient of G
is normalized to one. The returned `q[j]` multiply `t^(2*j-M)`.

Write the unknown second solution as

```text
P(t) = sum_(j=0)^(N-M) p[j]*t^(2*j-(N-M))/(2*i).
```

The Wronskian becomes a real rectangular linear system. A pair of indices
`j,l` contributes to row `k=j+l` with coefficient

```text
q[l] * sin((2*j-2*l-N+2*M)*gamma/2).
```

There are N+1 equations and N-M+1 unknowns. Complete pivoting selects a
square subset; Uni20 solves the original selected equations, together with
an identity right-hand side to estimate the inverse infinity norm. **Every
one of the N+1 equations is then checked.** This is not a least-squares fit,
and no normal equations or lower-precision conversions are used.

The binomial right-hand side is divided by its central coefficient, which
is generated by ratios without forming a large binomial integer. The
returned `p` includes that normalization. `residual_norm` is the maximum
absolute coefficient discrepancy against this unit-normalized RHS. It is
not divided by a possibly enormous P; large cancelling terms must not make
an inaccurate identity appear more accurate.

The statuses distinguish `consistent`, `inconsistent`, `ill_conditioned`,
and `nonfinite`. A pivot or reciprocal condition estimate at/below
`64*epsilon` is inconclusive. Invalid arguments throw. A caller supplies
the coefficient-residual tolerance explicitly when the default
`256*epsilon` is insufficient; the checker never loosens it automatically.
Continuation and Wronskian residuals have different conditioning and units.
In particular, a converged continuation can fail a tight Wronskian check
because the coordinate transform amplified coefficient errors.

## The phantom-root exception has a nonzero eigenvector

[Popkov, Zhang and Klümper](../CITATIONS.md#popkov-2021), equations (4),
(5), (11), and (12), construct periodic spin helices and their fixed-Sz
projections. Their Hamiltonian uses Pauli matrices and subtracts the
polarized energy. In our spin-1/2 convention their zero energy is `N*Delta/4`.

For our odd-ring collision, choose

```text
pitch = pi+pi/N, Delta_c=cos(pitch)=-cos(pi/N).
```

The pitch closes around the ring because `N*pitch` is an integer multiple
of `2*pi`. In the sector with M down spins, the projected state has amplitude

```text
psi(j_1,...,j_M) = exp(i*pitch*(j_1+...+j_M)) / sqrt(binomial(N,M))
```

on each ordered set of occupied sites. Every amplitude has equal nonzero
magnitude. Local divergence terms telescope on the ring, giving an
eigenvector with energy `N*Delta_c/4`. The opposite pitch gives its reflected
partner.

Our collision polynomial has all z roots at `-a=-tan(pi/(2*N))`, so its
Laurent transform is proportional to `t^M`: an endpoint coefficient
vanishes. These are infinite conventional rapidities, outside the finite-root
Wronskian test. Failure of that test here must **not** be interpreted as an
unphysical state. The returned `endpoint_ratio` records the smaller endpoint
coefficient relative to the largest coefficient, without imposing a universal
cutoff. Nor does existence of the helix eigenvector alone prove sector or
global minimality.

## Regression coverage and remaining work

Native fp64, long-double, and fp128 tests cover:

- Continued N=3,5,7,9 folded sectors at three generic negative couplings.
- N=13,17,21, `|Sz|=1/2`, at Delta=-0.999 with the default Wronskian tolerance.
- The analytic five-site two-magnon polynomial, affine-coordinate invariance,
  and direct evaluation of the Laurent identity at complex t.
- Rejection of the singular pair `z=+-i` on odd rings. Its cleared TQ equation
  is polynomial, but its physicality condition would require `1=(-1)^N`.
- A perturbed polynomial whose exact momentum constraint is preserved but
  whose Wronskian is inconsistent.
- Vanishing endpoint coefficients at the phantom collision, and direct
  spin-basis Hamiltonian action on both projected helices in **every** sector
  through N=9, with no double-precision ED oracle for those eigenvectors.
- The physical vacuum at Delta=-1/2: `q^3=1` makes the Laurent ansatz
  inconclusive even without occupied roots. Root-of-unity exceptions are
  not limited to the ground-branch collision.

A GCC 13 probe of the same larger branches at Delta=-0.97 illustrates the
conditioning issue. For N=21 the Wronskian residual is approximately
7.75e-13 (fp64), 4.11e-15 (long double), and 7.86e-30 (fp128).
All are small in absolute terms, but exceed the default `256*epsilon`
in their own types; the checker reports `inconsistent` at that tolerance.
The reciprocal condition estimate is about 9.79e-6 and the endpoint ratio
about 1.05e-9. These are numerical observations, not universal error bounds
or grounds for labelling the continued state unphysical. At Delta=-0.5 the
same probe returns `ill_conditioned`, consistent with the root-of-unity
restriction, rather than silently accepting a failed factorization.

The helper is not yet a continuation acceptance gate. Larger-chain error
amplification, other root-of-unity configurations, finite repeated roots,
and a precise admissibility policy still need work. Even an admissible
eigenstate can be an excited state. The internal
[all-sector scan](xxz-odd-continuation.md#comparing-sectors-without-assuming-the-answer)
now compares every followed branch and refuses to select an incomplete
minimum. Physical-state acceptance and reliable sector-minimum tracking
remain requirements for public odd-ring negative-Delta support.
